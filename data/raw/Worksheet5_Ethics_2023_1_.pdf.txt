This worksheet tries to get you thinking about the deeper aspects of right and wrong in 
software engineering. 
Attempt this worksheet in groups of 3-5 students. Try to see how different group members 
look at the given question in different angles relating to ethics. 
Then, present one of the answers in Q2 and Q3 to other groups and get their feedback. 
1. Motivation (10 minutes)
Come up with as many reasons as you can for why someone might behave unethically in a 
software project. 
2. Imagining Harm (45 minutes)
For each of the following systems, come up with a brief story that describes how a lapse of 
ethics/professionalism could lead to real harm to other people. 
In each case, note the following: 
• It may be easier to work backwards, first considering who could be harmed and how
(physically, financially, socially, psychologically, etc.), what involvement the software
might have in this, and what actual SE mistakes could lead to that.
• You may be able to come up with “nightmare scenarios”, but you could also describe
more subtle problems. Both are valid!
• Mix it up. Don’t just rewrite the scenarios from the lecture, for instance.
• Really bad things tend to happen due to very high level management mistakes, and/or
multiple mistakes piling up.
A single bug is generally not an ethical mistake itself, because we cannot reasonably avoid
them. In software engineering there are (or should be!) ways of fixing bugs before they
result in actual harm.
Here are the software systems to consider: 
(a) A video chat system.
(b) A software system for controlling traﬀic lights.
(c) An online banking system.
(d) A software package for helping to design houses.
(e) An aircraft autopilot.
3. Code of ethics ( 15 minutes)
Discuss how following either ACS code of ethics or IEEE-CS code of ethics could have
helpful to reduce the harm to other people you have identified in 2 above for two of the
software systems of your choice.
End of Worksheet
